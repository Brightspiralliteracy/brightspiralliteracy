Is this right for your child?

You already know something is off. Summer is when you do something about it.

The Summer Reading Intensive is for children ages 5 to 11 whose reading is not where it should be, for children who are capable of more than their current school is giving them, and for families who simply want to protect what the school year built. If you have been waiting for the right moment, this is it.

Reading time breaks the whole household
"She read an entire page out loud and couldn't tell me a single thing that happened. Then she said she was stupid."
That moment, when your child shuts down and turns it inward, is the moment most parents book the call. The decoding foundation is not solid. Everything else is built on top of it. Three weeks of targeted instruction changes what homework time looks like.
You're moving schools again
"We've moved twice. Every time there's a gap. I don't know how much he's actually lost."
Curriculum switches between IB, Cambridge, and American programs leave holes that nobody maps for you. The intensive pinpoints exactly where the gap is before the next school year begins, so you are not starting from scratch again in September.
The school says she's fine. You don't believe it.
"The teacher says she's within the normal range. But I can see something is wrong and I've been seeing it for months."
You are not overreacting. A reading specialist in a one-on-one lesson sees things a busy classroom teacher does not have the time or the training to catch. Three weeks gives you a clear answer and a specific plan, not another wait-and-see.
She's capable of more than she's showing
"She's so bright but reading is killing her confidence. She didn't used to hate it."
Confidence is not a personality trait in this context. It is a reading skill problem. When a child understands how words work, the dread lifts. The intensive builds the foundation that makes reading feel possible again instead of impossible.
Stuck in a reading level and not moving
"She's been on the same reading level at school for months. The teacher says she's fine but she's not growing."
School reading levels are a management tool, not a ceiling. When a child is stuck, it usually means there is a specific skill gap preventing them from moving forward. Three weeks of targeted instruction identifies exactly what that gap is and builds past it.

The Summer Reading Intensive is not tutoring homework support, catch-up cramming, or reading practice for its own sake. It is structured literacy instruction led by a qualified specialist. Every lesson is built on what your child specifically needs, not a generic program. If you are looking for someone to sit with your child while they read, this is not that.

The Bright Spiral Literacy Method

What actually happens across nine lessons

Every lesson is built on the Bright Spiral Literacy Method and grounded in the science of reading. The sequence is deliberate. The instruction is evidence-based. The outcomes are measurable.

Ground
Know the child before teaching them
Lesson one is devoted to getting to know your child as a person and completing the BSL Literacy Assessment. What they love, what makes them shut down, and where the exact gap is. Every lesson that follows is built on what we find here.
Build
Science of reading instruction, lesson by lesson
Lessons 2 through 8 are targeted structured literacy instruction built on the assessment findings. Phonemic awareness, phonics patterns, fluency, spelling. Adjusted each lesson based on what your child shows us. No generic program. No repeated worksheets.
Rise
Progress you can see and name
Progress in structured literacy is concrete and measurable, not a feeling. You will see it in the words your child stops guessing at, the sentences they read without stalling, the moment they stop dreading the page.
Shine
The BSL Reader Report
The BSL Literacy Assessment is repeated in lesson nine so progress is concrete, not just felt. Every family receives the BSL Reader Report at the close of the intensive, a specific document about your child that their new teacher can use from day one.

Minutes each

Questions

What parents ask before they book

The intensive is not only for children who are significantly behind. Some of the children who benefit most are bright kids who are managing but not building the solid foundation they need. They are guessing at unfamiliar words instead of decoding them. They are reading fluently in familiar texts but stalling on anything new. Some have been assigned a reading level at school and have not moved from it in months. School reading levels are a management tool, not a ceiling. When a child is stuck, there is usually a specific skill gap the school has not identified. Nine targeted lessons will find it and build past it before the next school year starts.
The intensive is designed as a fixed block for a reason. The lessons build on each other, and the momentum across three weeks is part of what makes it effective. If your family has a trip planned that overlaps with one of the rounds, the other round may work better. If you have a single unavoidable conflict mid-intensive, contact me directly and we will see what is possible. What I do not do is reschedule casually, because it disrupts the sequence and is unfair to other families in that round.
This is exactly what the intensive is built for. Every time a child moves schools, there is a risk of reading gaps that nobody maps for you. The BSL Reader Report you receive at the end of the intensive gives you a clear, specific picture of where your child is and what the new school needs to know. That document is something you can share with a new teacher, a school learning support coordinator, or a new tutor. It is the thing you wish you had had at every previous transition.
Bilingualism is not a reading problem, and I do not treat it as one. For some children, oral language in the home language is well ahead of reading in English, and that mismatch can look like a reading issue when it is not. For others, there are genuine gaps in the phonics foundation that the school has not addressed. The BSL Literacy Assessment in lesson one separates these things out clearly. The instruction is built on what we actually find, not on assumptions about bilingual learners.
Consider what your child is getting at school. In most international school classrooms, literacy instruction runs around 60 minutes per day, split across a teacher and possibly one assistant, among typically 20 children. That works out to roughly 3 minutes of direct instruction per child per day. Apps extend practice time but they cannot pinpoint the specific issue. They cannot tell you whether your child is guessing at words because of a phonemic awareness gap, a phonics pattern they missed, a fluency problem, or a confidence collapse that is masking everything else. A trained specialist can. The difference between an app and a qualified human in a one-on-one lesson is the difference between repeated exposure and precise instruction. Precise instruction is what moves a child forward. Generic tutors, even good ones, usually do not have the structured literacy training to do this. What makes the difference is a qualified person who knows the science, knows your child, and is building each lesson on what they found in the last one. That is what this is.
